# The Illusion of Thinking

The Illusion of Thinking is a 2025 research paper from Apple's machine learning researchers — Parshin Shojaee and colleagues — examining how large reasoning models (LRMs) actually behave as problem difficulty increases. It became one of the most discussed AI papers of the year.

The authors design controllable puzzle environments — the Tower of Hanoi, river crossing, blocks world, and checkers passing — where complexity can be dialed up precisely while keeping the rules fixed. Their findings: at low complexity, standard LLMs often outperform the elaborate "reasoning" models; at medium complexity, thinking models pull ahead; and past a critical threshold, accuracy collapses to zero even though the models have the tokens to keep going. Analysis of the models' traces shows they shift strategies, waste effort, and eventually give up — what the authors call the illusion of thinking.

The paper is a careful empirical contribution to the debate on whether these models truly "reason". Read it for its methodology and measured tone, and judge the conclusions yourself. An important data point in understanding the limits of current reasoning models.

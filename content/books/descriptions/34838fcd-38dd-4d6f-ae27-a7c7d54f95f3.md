# Foundations of Programming

Foundations of Programming: Building Better Software, by Karl Seguin, is a short free ebook aimed at developers moving from "code that works" to code that lasts. It distills practical software craftsmanship into a readable afternoon, with examples primarily in C#.

The opening chapters focus on the fundamentals of object-oriented design — encapsulation, abstractions, and avoiding common anti-patterns — along with a case for declarative, intention-revealing code. Later sections cover systems-level concerns: testability and unit testing, error handling, and a closing part on scalability that introduces caching, queuing, and database indexing at a gentle pace.

Its strength is proportion: it covers the ideas that matter most to working developers without drowning them in theory. If you are early in your career, it is an excellent map of what "writing good software" actually means in practice. Experienced developers will still find the scalability chapters a useful, compact refresher.

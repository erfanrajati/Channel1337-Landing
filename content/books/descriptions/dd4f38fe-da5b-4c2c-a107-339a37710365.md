# Scaling Viki

Scaling Viki, by Karl Seguin, is a short, free practitioner's account of scaling Viki — a video streaming site serving a global audience — as its traffic grew. Rather than theory, it is a worked case study of real infrastructure decisions made under real load.

The book walks through the bottlenecks a growing site actually hits, in the order it hits them: adding caching layers, moving from a single database to replication, then sharding, offloading static content to a CDN, moving slow work to background jobs and queues, and knowing when to buy managed services instead of building. Each step is motivated by the failure or pain point that triggered it, with candid notes on costs and mistakes.

The value is the shape of the journey — that scaling is a sequence of modest, targeted changes driven by measurement, not a big rewrite. Developers with no experience operating at scale will come away with a concrete mental model. It is the kind of war story that saves you a few of your own.

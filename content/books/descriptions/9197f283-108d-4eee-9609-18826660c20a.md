# Cryptography and Network Security

Cryptography and Network Security: Principles and Practice, 7th edition by William Stallings, is the standard textbook on the mathematics and protocols that keep communication private. It builds the field from first principles up to the systems used on the real internet.

The book is organized into two broad arcs. The first covers cryptographic algorithms: classical ciphers, block ciphers including DES and AES, pseudo-random number generation, asymmetric techniques such as RSA and elliptic curves, key distribution, and hash functions, MACs, and digital signatures. The second applies them to network security: user authentication, transport-layer security (TLS), IP security, email security, and stand-alone systems, with a final part on system-level threats like intruders, malware, and firewalls.

Stallings' style is rigorous but accessible, with worked examples, figures, and end-of-chapter problems supporting self-study. If you want to understand not just how to use cryptography but why protocols are designed the way they are, this remains one of the best foundations available.

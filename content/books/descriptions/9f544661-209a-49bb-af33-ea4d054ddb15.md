# Software Architecture Patterns

Software Architecture Patterns, by Mark Richards, is the O'Reilly short report that became a classic first stop for understanding common architectural styles. Its subtitle says it plainly: understanding common architecture patterns and when to use them.

The book examines five patterns in depth, one chapter each: layered architecture, the classic separation into presentation, business, and persistence tiers; event-driven architecture with its broker and mediator topologies; microkernel (plug-in) architecture; microservices; and space-based architecture built on replicated in-memory data grids. Every chapter follows the same disciplined format — a description, a worked example, and a candid analysis of the pattern's strengths and weaknesses against metrics like agility, testability, performance, and scalability.

That consistent trade-off analysis is the book's real lesson: no pattern wins everywhere, and the architect's job is matching pattern to context. At its short length, it is the fastest way to build a working vocabulary of architecture styles. Read it before the thicker tomes — it makes them all easier to digest.

# Fundamentals of Software Architecture

Fundamentals of Software Architecture, by Mark Richards and Neal Ford, is the O'Reilly field guide to what architects actually do. It bridges the gap between writing code and making the large, expensive, long-lived decisions that shape a system.

The first half builds the vocabulary: the role of the architect, architectural thinking, and architectural characteristics — the "ilities" like scalability, elasticity, and availability — along with how to measure and trade them against each other. The second half surveys the major styles, including layered, pipeline, microkernel, service-based, event-driven, space-based, and microservices architectures, analyzing each by deployment, performance, and testability. Closing chapters cover architecture decisions, analysis techniques like ATAM, and the soft skills of negotiation and team leadership.

Its key contribution is trade-off analysis: every structure is presented with strengths and weaknesses rather than as a silver bullet. New architects get a complete curriculum; experienced ones get a shared language for design discussions. One of the few books that treats architecture as a discipline of its own.
